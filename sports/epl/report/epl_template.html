Glossaryjargon used above, in plain English
xG (expected goals)
A number representing how good a team's scoring chances were in a match, regardless of whether they actually scored - a shot from close range with no defenders counts for more xG than a long-range speculative effort. More stable match-to-match than actual goals.
npxG
Non-penalty xG - the same idea, with penalty-kick chances excluded (penalties are converted at a very different, more predictable rate than open play).
Elo rating
A single number representing team strength, going up when a team beats a stronger opponent and down when it loses to a weaker one - same family of rating chess uses.
Dixon-Coles model
A statistical adjustment (Dixon & Coles, 1997) to a plain goals-based model that corrects for real football matches having slightly more low-scoring draws (0-0, 1-1) than a naive model expects.
Monte Carlo simulation
Instead of computing an exact answer with one formula, the model plays out the match thousands of times with realistic randomness and counts how often each outcome happens.
Moneyline / three-way market
A bet on the match result: home win, draw, or away win - unlike MLB, a draw is a real possible outcome here, not just win/loss.
Matchday
The Premier League's round number for the current fixture list (all 20 clubs play once per matchday across a normal weekend).
